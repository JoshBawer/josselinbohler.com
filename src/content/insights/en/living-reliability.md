Reliability teams do not lack documents. They lack continuity between them.

An FMECA describes what can fail. An RCA reconstructs why an event happened. RCM turns risk into tasks. Weibull analysis reveals how failure behaviour changes over time. Yet these methods are usually stored in separate files, built by different teams and allowed to age independently.

<p class="manifesto-line">The strategy should not be a collection of snapshots. It should be a maintained model of how the asset behaves.</p>

## One causal backbone

A useful reliability model links a small number of concepts:

**Function → functional failure → failure mode → mechanism → cause → consequence → control.**

Each method is then a different view of that shared model. An RCA can challenge an existing cause. Work-order history can change the likelihood assigned to a failure mode. A modified maintenance task can be traced back to the risk it controls. When those links are explicit, engineering knowledge can be reviewed instead of merely accumulated.

## Evidence before automation

Industrial AI is most valuable when it improves the discipline of the work: finding relevant evidence, identifying contradictions, proposing testable hypotheses and keeping traceability intact.

It should not hide uncertainty or issue an engineering conclusion without a reviewable chain of reasoning. The engineer remains accountable for acceptance, rejection and implementation. The system makes that judgement faster to prepare and easier to audit.

## A strategy that learns

A living strategy creates a feedback loop:

1. Operational events and condition data enter as evidence.
2. The causal model highlights what the evidence supports or challenges.
3. Engineers review the impact on risk, tasks and spares.
4. Approved changes flow back into execution systems.
5. Outcomes are measured and become new evidence.

This does not require a perfect digital twin. It starts with consistent identifiers, clear ownership and a model small enough for the team to maintain.

## The practical test

A reliability strategy is alive when a new failure can update more than a presentation. It can change the model, expose the affected controls and show which decision needs review.

That is the direction behind Reliability Factory: focused tools that connect engineering methods around evidence and judgement, without pretending that software replaces field experience.
